Donut charts

Four directions for the ten part-to-whole charts on the Accounts, Centres and Prospects tabs. Each shows one category chart (Industry) and one range chart (Revenue range) side by side, the way the tabs lay them out. Hover a ring or a row to link the two.

A. Today

For reference. The orange steps today are only a few shades apart, so neighbouring slices look alike. The labels sit outside on connector lines and fight for room. The total and each slice's figure only appear in the tooltip.

A2. Today, refined

New

Same chart, same labels outside the ring, nothing added beside it. The labels now line up in two clean columns instead of floating at odd spots, each with a short bent connector. The name reads in full ink and the share sits next to it in a heavier weight. The centre stays empty. Hovering a slice dims the other slices and labels, so its own label stands out.

A3. Today, refined, share first

As A2, but each label puts the share on top and the name underneath in a quieter grey, so a glance down either column reads the numbers first. Takes slightly more height per label.

B. Ring with a ranked list

Recommended

A thin ring with the total in the middle, beside a list that reads like a table: name, count and share, largest first. With one hue, the list carries each slice's name and figures, so the ring only has to give the shape, and a deeper orange means a bigger slice, fading to yellow for the small ones. Hovering a row lights its arc and swaps the centre to that slice. No labels on the ring, no connector lines, no tooltip needed.

  • Today's orange-to-yellow scheme, cut from nine near-identical shades to six that sit far enough apart to tell neighbours apart. The brand orange #f17c1d stays the anchor and "Others" keeps the pale cream, always last
  • Category charts: the largest slice gets the deepest orange, fading to yellow. Range charts: yellow to orange in range order, starting at 12 o'clock
  • A 2px gap between slices keeps them separate even where two steps sit close
  • The list is a real list of buttons, so it works by keyboard and replaces the right-click label menu

B2. Ring with a ranked list and share bars

Same as B with a hairline bar under each row. Close values (Healthcare against Manufacturing) become easy to tell apart. Costs a little height: rows go from 36 to 44px.

C. Large ring with a readout

The ring is the hero. The centre names the largest slice by default and follows the hover. Shares sit in a compact legend underneath. Closest to today's card shape, but counts only show in the centre, one at a time.

D. Focus on one slice

Every slice is pale yellow except the one that matters, in brand orange. Click a row to move the focus. Answers "what share is Technology" instantly, and keeps the page calm when ten donuts sit on one tab, at the cost of telling the other slices apart.
