Options for the two charts in the detail dialogs. Tech stack options show a real small centre next to the real Capgemini SE account. Revenue figures are sample data.

Technology stack (account and centre details)

The same treemap sits in both dialogs. On a centre every tool appears once, so all tiles weigh the same and the sizes say nothing. On an account a tool is counted once per centre that uses it, so size means "used at more centres". The big case below is the real Capgemini SE stack: 43 tools in 20 categories across 15 centres. Today the smaller categories collapse into slivers with no readable labels.

T2. Treemap, refined

Recommended

Still a treemap, laid out as near-square tiles at both levels instead of thin slices. Each category is a quiet grey tray with its name at the top and its tools inside, busiest category first, with more space between trays than between tiles. A tool's shade shows how many centres use it; hovering a tile outlines it and shows the tool name with its share of the stack. The box grows as tall as it needs so tool names fit, wrapping onto two lines where a tile is narrow: a centre with 3 tools stays short, a big account gets a taller box (up to 760px). A tool used at one centre is never a sliver: sizes still follow centre count, softened so the smallest tools keep room for a name. On a single centre every tile has the same shade, because every count is 1.

R2. Line, refined

Same line in the chart orange, with a flat light fill instead of the gradient and three quiet gridlines. The latest year is called out in the header with its change against the year before, and each point carries its figure.

R3. Columns

Recommended

Annual totals are separate amounts, so columns compare them most directly. Each column has its figure on top, the latest year is the deep orange and earlier years step paler, and the header gives the latest figure and its change. No axis needed.
